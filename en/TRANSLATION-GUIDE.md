# Translation Guide: Social AI Toolkit (NL to EN)

This guide is the single source of truth for all translators working on the English edition of the Social AI Toolkit. Follow it exactly.

---

## A. Voice & Tone Guidelines

### Who is Joost?

Joost MF Liebregts designs systems that are owned by the people they serve. Bottom-up, AI-augmented. He is not a facilitator (never use that as his title), not an expert, not a guru. He designs conditions under which people collaborate. AI is an amplifier, not the core.

### The voice formula

**Systemic insight + Playful presentation + Real vulnerability = Authentic Joost**

In English, this means:
- **Warm but not sweet.** Joost sounds like someone explaining something interesting over coffee, not like a TED talk.
- **Clear but not clinical.** Simple language, but with depth. Not corporate, not academic.
- **First person throughout.** "I noticed...", "What I see is...", "In my experience..."
- **Light self-deprecation preserved.** "Honestly, I don't think I have the answer" stays honest and humble.
- **Exploratory, not prescriptive.** "What if..." over "You should..." Always.

### The humility pattern

Joost suggests, never states. Especially on big questions.

| Dutch original | BAD English | GOOD English |
|----------------|-------------|--------------|
| "Volgens mij zit daar iets fundamenteels." | "This is fundamentally important." | "I think there's something fundamental here." |
| "Die vragen houden mij ook bezig." | "Those are the wrong questions." | "Those questions keep me up at night too." |
| "Wat ik merk in workshops..." | "Research shows that..." | "What I notice in workshops..." |
| "Eerlijk gezegd denk ik niet dat ik het antwoord heb." | "The answer is complex." | "Honestly, I don't think I have the answer." |

### The perspective pattern: situation=you, experience=I

| Context | Perspective | Why |
|---------|------------|-----|
| **Situation** (universal) | "you" | Reader sees themselves |
| **Experience/tendency** | "I" / "my" | Personal, not prescriptive |
| **Insight** | "I" | Honest sharing, not lecturing |

**Example translation:**

Dutch: "Je faciliteert een gesprek. De energie zakt. Mijn neiging was om zelf een vraag te stellen. Wat ik leerde: soms helpt het om de stilte te laten."

Good EN: "You're facilitating a conversation. The energy drops. My tendency was to jump in with a question. What I learned: sometimes it helps to let the silence be."

### Reflecting and learning

Never translate reflections as conclusions. Joost shares his thinking process.

| Dutch | BAD English | GOOD English |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| "Toen ik hierop reflecteerde..." | "The takeaway is..." | "When I reflected on this..." |
| "Wat ik merk is..." | "It is clear that..." | "What I notice is..." |
| "Dit laat me zien dat..." | "This proves that..." | "This shows me that..." |
| "Volgens mij is de vraag..." | "The question is..." | "The way I see it, the question is..." |

### Enthusiasm in writing

Joost uses understatement in writing, even when excited.

| Dutch | English |
|-------|---------|
| "Dat was wel mooi" | "That was something" / "That was quite something" |
| "Het interessante is..." | "Here's what's interesting..." |
| "Dat werkte best goed" | "That actually worked pretty well" |

Never translate with hype language: "game-changer", "revolutionary", "mind-blowing", "powerful".

### Introductions to tension

Joost unpacks tension step by step, letting the reader arrive at the conclusion. Then makes it explicit: "And so..."

Preserve this bottom-up discovery structure. Don't front-load conclusions.

### Anti-patterns (NEVER produce these in translation)

- "Here's what I learned. Take what resonates." (YouTube structure)
- "I don't believe in X" (Joost says what he DOES believe, not what he doesn't)
- "The truth is..." (absolute claims)
- "Game-changer" / "Revolutionary" (hype language)
- "This one thing changed everything" (clickbait)
- "You need to..." / "This is how it works" (prescriptive)
- "Stakeholders" when "participants" or "the people involved" works
- Excessive em-dashes. Use sparingly, only for rhetorical turns.

## F. Markdown Preservation Rules

### Structure (must match exactly)

- **Header levels** — Keep identical. `##` stays `##`.
- **Horizontal rules** — Keep `---` in same positions.
- **Blockquotes** — Keep `>` formatting.
- **Code blocks** — Keep ``` blocks unchanged (see section D).
- **Tables** — Translate content, preserve structure.
- **Lists** — Translate content, preserve bullet/number style.
- **Bold/italic** — Preserve emphasis on equivalent words.
- **Checkbox lists** — `- [ ]` stays `- [ ]`, translate the text.

### Images

- **Paths** — Keep unchanged: `images/filename.png`
- **Alt text** — Translate to English
- **Example:** `![Echo-knop in actie](images/echo-button.png)` becomes `![Echo button in action](images/echo-button.png)`

### Internal links

- Translate link text to English
- Map link paths to EN equivalents (see Link Mapping Table below)
- **Example:** `[Hoe mens en AI samenwerken](hoe.md)` becomes `[How people and AI collaborate](en/how.md)`

### Navigation lines

Translate navigation at bottom of pages:

`← [Terug naar Fase 2: Verdieping](../../fase-2-verdieping.md)` becomes
`← [Back to Phase 2: Deepening](../../en/phase-2-deepening.md)`

Adjust relative paths as needed for the `en/` directory structure.

## H. Translation Examples (Real Sentences)

These examples show how to translate actual sentences from the toolkit. Study them carefully.

### From waarom.md

**Dutch:**
> AI is overal. Je hebt er waarschijnlijk al mee gewerkt. Een vraag gesteld, een idee voorgelegd, een berg gedachten omgezet in iets waar je mee verder kon.

**Good English:**
> AI is everywhere. You've probably already worked with it. Asked a question, floated an idea, turned a heap of thoughts into something you could actually use.

**Bad English:**
> AI is ubiquitous. Most people have already utilized it for tasks like question-answering, idea validation, and thought organization.

---

**Dutch:**
> Die vragen houden mij ook bezig. En eerlijk gezegd denk ik niet dat ik het antwoord heb. Maar ondertussen is er iets anders gaande.

**Good English:**
> Those questions keep me up at night too. And honestly, I don't think I have the answer. But meanwhile, something else is happening.

**Bad English:**
> These are important questions that deserve consideration. While the answers remain elusive, there are other developments worth noting.

---

**Dutch:**
> Vanaf waar ik zat, zag ik de groepsdynamiek veranderen.

**Good English:**
> From where I was sitting, I saw the group dynamic shift.

**Bad English:**
> The group dynamics underwent a notable transformation at that point.

---

### From hoe.md

**Dutch:**
> Het werkte omdat het op het juiste *moment* kwam. De groep zat vast, de energie zakte, en precies toen kwam er een vraag die ze hielp verder te komen.

**Good English:**
> It worked because it came at the right *moment*. The group was stuck, the energy was dropping, and right then a question arrived that helped them move forward.

**Bad English:**
> Its effectiveness was attributable to optimal timing. When group energy decreased, the strategically placed question enabled forward progress.

---

**Dutch:**

**Good English:**
> The point isn't that AI can't make a plan — it absolutely can. The point is that ownership emerges when we put something of ourselves into it.

**Bad English:**
> While AI demonstrates plan-creation capabilities, the critical factor is human ownership through personal investment.

---

## I. Sentence Rhythm Rules

Joost uses short sentences for emphasis. Fragments are intentional. Preserve them.

**Dutch:** "Niet door te beslissen. Niet door samen te vatten. Maar door terug te spiegelen."
**English:** "Not by deciding. Not by summarizing. But by reflecting back."

**Dutch:** "Dat is waar ik naartoe wil. Daar gaat deze toolkit over."
**English:** "That's where I want to go. That's what this toolkit is about."

Never merge short sentences into longer academic ones. The rhythm is the voice.

---

## J. Closing Statements

### How Joost ends sections

Joost often closes with an invitation, not a conclusion. Preserve this pattern.

**Dutch:** "Dit is mijn perspectief, gebaseerd op mijn ervaring met AI in participatieve processen. Geen universele waarheid, maar een uitnodiging om mee te denken."

**English:** "This is my perspective, based on my experience with AI in participatory processes. Not a universal truth, but an invitation to think along."

Never close with "In conclusion..." or "To sum up..." or "Key takeaways:".
